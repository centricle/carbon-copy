Carbon Copy

Keeps a copy of what you submit in job applications and other web forms, as plain files on your own disk.

Nothing is recorded until you click Start recording at the bottom of this page. Here is what that turns on.

What is recorded

When you submit a form on a recorded site: each question as the page labels it, the answer you gave, and the name, size, type and SHA-256 hash of any file you attach. The file is read inside the page to compute the hash; its contents are never stored. Each record also keeps the page’s address and title.

On which sites

The candidate pages of these job application systems: Greenhouse and Lever job boards, Ashby, Workday, Workable, SmartRecruiters, the /jobs/ pages of iCIMS and the /careers/ pages of BambooHR.

Anywhere else, only after you open the toolbar popup on that site and choose Record this site. Chrome asks for permission one site at a time.

When

A record is made when you click a button labeled Submit, Apply or Send, or press Enter to submit, after filling something in. A submission the browser blocks for a missing answer stays a draft.

To stop for a while, choose Pause in the toolbar popup. Nothing is recorded for the hour or eight hours you pick, then recording resumes by itself.

What is skipped

  • Passwords and one-time codes
  • Card, bank and tax ID numbers, Social Security and social insurance numbers, and dates of birth, recognized by their labels
  • The number in a passport, driver’s license or national ID field
  • Any answer that is entirely a card number, a nine-digit number (the shape of an SSN or a routing number) or an IBAN, whatever the field is called
  • EEO self-identification answers (gender, race, veteran and disability status), unless you turn them on in the popup

These rules are a safety net, not a guarantee. A secret written out in words is recorded like any other answer.

Drafts stay in the browser

While you type, a draft is kept in the browser so a crash or a closed tab does not lose your work. Drafts are never written to disk on their own; only Save files or Export all in the popup does that. They stay in the browser until you remove the extension.

Where the files go

Each submission becomes two files in Downloads/carbon-copy/: a Markdown file to read and a JSON file for scripts. Chrome may show them in its downloads bubble as they save.

If Chrome is set to Ask where to save each file before downloading (Settings, Downloads), you will see two save dialogs per submission. Turn that setting off to save without asking.

Nothing leaves your device

Carbon Copy makes no network requests. No account, no server, no analytics. Read the full privacy policy.
